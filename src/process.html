Process

Routing runs in this browser. Place search uses keyless Photon and the map uses OpenFreeMap vector tiles from OpenStreetMap data. Your people and settings stay on this device.

Starts matched to nearby stops

Each start place is matched to nearby transit stops within a fixed walking radius. A stop saved from the local list uses the stop itself; an address or map pin uses the closest stops around that point. Selecting a single mode restricts candidate stops to that mode's kinds; stops are grouped by station, and at most 12 nearby sources are used for each start.

Walk tolerance per person

Every person has a walk tolerance from 0 to 60 minutes in 5-minute steps, set on their card with a default of 20 minutes. When a place is within that walking time of a start, its walking time is used for that person instead of a transit time. Changing the tolerance re-groups fair areas from the travel times already computed; it does not change which transit paths exist. Walk tolerance stays per person. When a direct walk fits a person's tolerance it is shown instead of transit; a faster transit route may still exist. Areas are compared by the active metric: travel time in Time mode or fares in Fare mode. The Parameters panel has one global Include average headway checkbox, saved with settings and share links; it controls whether the interchange wait below is charged and shown.

Multi-source Dijkstra

Those nearby stops become the sources of a multi-source Dijkstra search over a local transit graph. Walking links connect the sources, and ride edges follow the scheduled network. Each boarding adds a fixed penalty (one minute on rail, two minutes on bus). When the Include average headway parameter is on, the solver also adds up to half of the day's average time between services, weighted by how long the ride lasts, but only when a ride follows another transit leg: the first boarding assumes the traveller times their departure, so it has no headway wait. With the parameter off, no headway wait is charged anywhere. Every place reachable within the 90-minute cap gets a travel time for each person.

Fair-cell grouping

The map is divided into cells on a fixed latitude/longitude grid of about 0.005°. Disparity across everyone only filters cells against the tolerance; it does not group them. Surviving cells are ranked by a stored fit score built from the whole-minute longest and spread (not itself displayed), with the displayed longest and then the displayed spread as tie-breaks. Fare mode orders the surviving cells by the displayed fare gap and highest fare instead. The displayed spread and longest values are whole-minute times. The tolerance control changes which cells survive without re-running the whole search.

Route legs

Route legs follow the shapes stored in the transit feed. Walking segments connect a start to a stop, a stop to a ride, and the last ride to the meeting area. Ride segments trace the actual path of the MTR or bus line where a detailed shape exists; short or missing shapes fall back to straight segments between stops. MTR legs use the full line name, such as Tsuen Wan Line, Kwun Tong Line or Tuen Ma Line, instead of a three-letter code. Each ride chip shows the stops as Start → End with a Unicode arrow. Every ride leg includes its fixed boarding penalty; when the headway parameter is on, a leg boarded after another transit leg also carries its interchange wait, shown as a whole-minute note such as Interchange wait 4 min in the leg details. The first ride leg has no such note, and no wait note appears when the parameter is off. Every minute value is shown as a whole number. Access walks include a fixed 1-minute buffer so a short hop does not round to zero; the final walk to a pinned point uses raw distance without that buffer.

Fare estimates

Fares are adult Octopus fares looked up from the MTR's published station-pair fare matrix, bundled from MTR open data as a build-time snapshot; Airport Express uses its own published table. Bus and ferry fares come from the Transport Department full-fare snapshot and are shown as exact. When two candidate Transport Department records tie on the endpoint-match score, the generator deliberately selects the higher of those full fares for the line; that is a disclosed tie-breaking policy, not a measured fare. Only fares with no snapshot entry use a distance band and are marked ≈. Some route numbers cannot be matched to a published fare and therefore use the marked band fallback. Fares affect the active objective in Fare mode but never which areas are time-feasible.

Data sources

The transit graph comes from a community-maintained Hong Kong GTFS feed. The interest index uses open Hong Kong government data and Overture. Interest ratings count source feed rows as per-row proxies, not deduplicated venues, and are informational only. Place search uses Photon, and the basemap uses OpenFreeMap vector tiles from OpenStreetMap data.
